# Writing the PR/FAQ

Write as if the product launched today and a journalist will read only the first paragraph. Plain words, concrete numbers, no internal jargon. Where an adjective stands in for a fact ("fast", "easy", "affordable"), replace it with the number or scene behind it ("in 30 seconds", "with one photo", "$9 a month").

Amazon's excerpt of *Working Backwards* says the press release is a few paragraphs and always under one page. The book asks for an FAQ of about five pages or fewer. For a smoke test, shorter is better: the FAQ exists to expose the riskiest assumption, not to look complete.

Section names below follow common Amazon-style templates. They vary between sources; the order matters more than the labels.

## Template

```markdown
> Draft written as if launched. Not a real announcement.

# [Headline: product name + the customer benefit, one line]

## [Subheadline: who it is for and what they get, one sentence]

**[City], [launch date]** — [Summary: what it is, who it helps, the main benefit.
Written so a reader who stops here still knows what launched.]

[Problem: the customer's problem in their own words. What they do today and
what that costs them in time, money, or worry.]

[Solution: how the product removes that problem. One concrete scene of a
customer using it. What they put in, what they get back, how long it takes.]

"[Quote from the company: why we built it, in one or two sentences.]"
— [Name, role]

[Getting started: where to go, what it costs, what the first step is.]

"[Imagined customer quote: the outcome in the customer's words.]"
— [Imagined customer, description] *(imagined for this draft)*

[Call to action: one sentence and a link.]

---

## External FAQ

Questions a customer would ask before trying it. Typical ones: How much does it
cost and why? What do I need to provide? When do I get the result? What if it
doesn't work for me? What happens to my data? How is this different from
[what I use today]?

**Q:** ...
**A:** ...

## Internal FAQ

Questions the team or an investor would ask. Typical ones:

- Who exactly is the customer, and how many of them can we reach?
- What do they use today, and why would they switch?
- What does one sale earn after the cost of making and delivering it?
- What does it cost to acquire a customer through the first channel?
- What must be true technically, legally, or operationally, and what is unknown?
- **What is the riskiest assumption?** If it is false, the product fails.
- Why now? What are we choosing not to do?

**Q:** ...
**A:** ...

---

## Verdict

Compelling / not yet / no. Weakest line: "...". Why: ...
```

## Rules

- Mark the draft. The first line says it describes an imagined launch.
- Keep the imagined customer quote inside this document. It never travels to a landing page or ad.
- Write unknown prices, dates, and numbers as `[TBD]` or as a labeled guess ("guess: $9/month, based on ..."). An honest gap is more useful than an invented figure.
- Name one riskiest assumption in the internal FAQ. That is the assumption the three angles in step 3 must speak to.
- After the test, update this document in place. Add the decision, the counts, and the date under the verdict. Keep the original version of any number you changed.
